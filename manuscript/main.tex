\begin{abstract}
Time-resolved direct simulation Monte Carlo (DSMC) fields are used to determine whether a detached rarefied hypersonic bow shock contains a dynamically meaningful displacement coordinate that can be separated from correlated molecular sampling fluctuations. The configuration is Mach-10 nitrogen flow over a circular cylinder for $0.01\leq\KnD\leq1$. A density half-jump centre is extracted on body-normal rays, unsupported solid-side points are removed before shock-attached interpolation, and temporal coarse graining is performed before feature extraction. The marker covariance is represented as the sum of a persistent autoregressive component and a correlated sampling component. Corrected field proper orthogonal decomposition is high rank at every Knudsen number, yet a weak collective coordinate is resolved independently at $\KnD=0.01$ and $0.025$. Over this interval the mean 10--90 density width increases by $82\%$, while the physical angular eigenfunction retains an absolute normalized inner product of $0.972$ and the convective memory changes only from $\tau_p^*=0.653$ to $0.724$. The memory persists for approximately $6.5$--$10.6$ layer-passage times and $2.8$--$3.1$ standoff-passage times, identifying a slow global response rather than local marker jitter. Full-field matched filtering recovers the same motion from density and pressure, with marker correlations $(0.946,0.901)$ at $\KnD=0.01$ and $(0.790,0.727)$ at $\KnD=0.025$. A new moment-participation analysis shows that density and pressure remain strongly coupled to the displacement as the layer broadens, whereas Mach number and translational temperature progressively decouple. Thus early rarefaction preserves the angular geometry and slow memory of the collective coordinate while weakening thermodynamic synchronization. At $\KnD\geq0.05$, statistical power deteriorates too rapidly to establish physical disappearance. The result is a noise-separated, multi-moment validation of a slow collective coordinate embedded in broadband kinetic fluctuations, distinct from the mean shock-layer inflation and parameter-space similarity considered in the companion study.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Statement of significance}
The companion mean-flow study of the same cylinder family quantifies rarefaction-induced standoff growth, layer broadening and variable-dependent parameter-space similarity \citep{RoohiShoja2026Mean}. The present work asks a different fluid-mechanical question: does the broadened bow layer possess a slow collective coordinate in time? This distinction is not semantic. Mean profiles can broaden smoothly even when instantaneous motion is dominated by particle sampling, and a large POD mode can be created artificially if a registered window enters the solid body. After eliminating that support artifact, the instantaneous fields are strongly high dimensional. Nevertheless, two independent near-continuum records contain the same long-range angular displacement shape. More importantly, the shape persists while the mean density layer almost doubles in width. The collective memory is several times longer than either the local layer-passage or standoff-passage time, and the signal is recovered independently from density, pressure, Mach number and translational temperature. The new physical result is therefore a separation between static broadening and dynamic organization: early rarefaction preserves a slow angular displacement geometry but progressively reduces the synchronous participation of the thermodynamic moments. The coordinate is weak in raw variance and is not interpreted as an instability. It is a slow, noise-excited response embedded within broadband kinetic fluctuations. This provides a dynamic counterpart to the mean-flow paper and a controlled framework for distinguishing physical coherent motion from correlated sampling noise in particle simulations.

\section{Introduction}
Hypersonic flow over a blunt body produces a detached compression layer in which momentum redirection, translational heating, internal-energy relaxation and gas--surface interaction occur over coupled spatial scales. When the molecular mean free path is no longer negligible relative to the body dimension, the layer has finite kinetic thickness and the continuum notion of a discontinuous shock surface becomes operational rather than exact. DSMC remains the reference particle method in this regime \citep{Bird1994,Lofthouse2007}, and the circular cylinder remains a canonical geometry because it contains a curved detached front, a stagnation region and strong wall interaction without geometric complexity.

Most cylinder studies have focused on mean flow, force, heating, slip or continuum breakdown. Time-resolved particle fields pose a different problem. \citet{Stefanov2000} demonstrated that macroscopic fluctuations around a rarefied hypersonic cylinder are substantial and spatially non-uniform. More recent kinetic studies have extracted coherent structures from shock/boundary-layer interactions, separated ramps and hypersonic boundary layers \citep{Sawant2021,Karpuzcu2025,Klothakis2021}. These works establish that kinetic snapshots contain physically organized dynamics, but they also emphasize that coherent-structure extraction from particle data cannot be separated from sampling statistics.

The specific question here is whether the detached bow layer possesses a collective displacement coordinate. In a continuum description, translation of a thin front produces the familiar first-order disturbance
\begin{equation}
 q'(s,\theta,t)\simeq-a(\theta,t)\frac{\partial \mean q}{\partial s},
 \label{eq:translation}
\end{equation}
where $s$ is distance from the wall along a body-normal ray. If the bow shock moves collectively, $a(\theta,t)$ should contain a slowly varying angular component shared by distant portions of the front. In DSMC, however, the observed marker also contains finite-particle noise, and the noise is temporally correlated because particles and sampling accumulators persist between neighbouring outputs. A raw POD, DMD or SPOD decomposition can therefore promote sampling structure into an apparently coherent mode.

Two additional complications are important. First, registration methods remove transport-dominated variability and can greatly improve reduced-order compactness \citep{RowleyMarsden2000,Reiss2018,Taddei2020}. Their validity depends on the registration map and its support. A shock-attached grid that crosses a solid boundary is not benign: interpolation can fill a non-physical region with repeated wall-adjacent values and generate artificial low rank. Second, POD and dynamic modes answer different questions. POD identifies variance-optimal structures \citep{Lumley1967,Sirovich1987,Berkooz1993,Taira2017}; DMD and SPOD target time evolution and frequency-dependent coherence \citep{Schmid2010,Towne2018}. None of them, by themselves, distinguishes physical covariance from particle sampling covariance.

A related theoretical development is kinetic linear stability theory. \citet{Karpuzcu2026} linearized the Boltzmann--BGK equation about one-dimensional kinetic normal-shock base states and showed that non-Maxwellian velocity distributions shift continuous spectral branches toward less stable locations. That analysis concerns deterministic infinitesimal perturbations of planar argon shocks up to Mach 4, whereas the present work concerns stochastic fluctuations of a curved, body-coupled nitrogen bow shock at Mach 10. The two are therefore not direct validations of one another. They do, however, motivate a useful interpretation: a linearly stable kinetic response can be continuously excited by molecular forcing, so a reproducible covariance mode need not be an unstable eigenmode.

The companion study of this cylinder family \citep{RoohiShoja2026Mean} is available as arXiv:2605.17099. It examines mean standoff, mean 10--90 thickness, variable-specific relaxation lengths and parameter-indexed shock-attached POD. Those results establish that the mean compression layer inflates and loses single-scale similarity as rarefaction increases. None of those parameter-space results establishes that the layer moves collectively in time. The present paper uses the time-resolved nitrogen outputs and addresses that dynamic counterpart. The only mean quantities reused are the half-jump position and width required to define the instantaneous coordinate; all covariance, memory, power, phase-portrait and full-field displacement results are new. The present paper makes four contributions. First, it audits the support of the shock-attached map and quantifies false low-rank structures created by solid-side interpolation. Second, it applies temporal coarse graining before marker extraction and infers a physical covariance in the presence of correlated sampling noise. Third, it identifies the same long-range angular displacement shape in independent $\KnD=0.01$ and $0.025$ records. Fourth, it validates the coordinate in the complete density, Mach-number, pressure and translational-temperature fields through the translation template in \cref{eq:translation}. The central result is deliberately narrow: a weak collective coordinate exists in the resolved near-continuum cases. The present data do not have sufficient power to prove that it disappears as rarefaction increases. Three physical hypotheses organize the analysis. First, a true displacement response must survive temporal coarse graining and remain coherent over angular separations much larger than one sampling cell. Second, if early rarefaction merely broadens the mean layer without changing its global dynamics, the normalized angular mode and convective memory should remain approximately invariant between $\KnD=0.01$ and $0.025$. Third, if different moments cease to move synchronously, the equivalent displacement amplitudes inferred from density, pressure, Mach number and temperature should exhibit a systematic participation hierarchy even while the marker mode remains detectable.

\section{Configuration and analysis}
\subsection{DSMC data and nondimensionalization}
The configuration is two-dimensional Mach-10 nitrogen flow over a circular cylinder of diameter $D=\SI{0.3048}{m}$. The freestream and wall temperatures are $T_\infty=\SI{200}{K}$ and $T_w=\SI{500}{K}$. Gas--gas collisions use the variable-hard-sphere model and the standard DS2V rotational nonequilibrium treatment; vibrational excitation and chemistry are not included. The Knudsen number is
\begin{equation}
 \KnD=\frac{\lambda_\infty}{D},
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_\infty$ is the freestream mean free path. Time is reported as $\tstar=tU_\infty/D$.

The geometry, molecular model and statistically converged mean fields are shared with the companion mean-flow paper \citep{RoohiShoja2026Mean}, but the data object analysed here is different. The companion work treats one converged field per operating condition and asks how the parameterized mean family collapses. Here each operating condition supplies a consecutive temporal record, and no parameter-to-parameter POD mode is interpreted dynamically. This separation is important because a compact parameterized mean family may coexist with high-dimensional instantaneous fluctuations, while a weak coherent coordinate can remain invisible to energy-ranked field POD.

Nine Knudsen numbers are considered. Every case contains at least 200 consecutive macroscopic fields; the longer records are used for stationarity, covariance and power analyses, whereas exactly 200 snapshots are used for direct field-POD comparison. The output cadence is not forced to be identical in physical time, so all temporal fits use the measured $\Delta \tstar$ of each record. \Cref{tab:data} summarizes the data and the mean density half-jump geometry extracted below.

\subsection{Half-jump marker and physical shock-attached support}
The upper upstream sector is represented by 60 body-normal rays over $120^\circ\leq\theta\leq179^\circ$. On each ray, $s=r-R$ measures distance from the cylinder wall. Robust upstream and downstream density plateaux define the normalized transition
\begin{equation}
 \rho^*(s,\theta,t)=\frac{\rho-\rho_{up}}{\rho_{down}-\rho_{up}}.
\end{equation}
The centre $\scenter(\theta,t)$ is the $\rho^*=0.5$ crossing and $\deltaten(\theta,t)$ is the distance between the 0.1 and 0.9 crossings. The attached coordinate is
\begin{equation}
 \xi=\frac{s-\scenter}{\deltaten}, \qquad -1\leq\xi\leq4.
 \label{eq:xi}
\end{equation}
The common attached grid contains 260 points in $\xi$. A point is retained only when $s/R\geq0.02$; unsupported points are masked rather than filled from the nearest gas cell. \Cref{fig:geometry} illustrates the map, the 10--50--90 construction and a time-resolved marker field.

A preliminary registration route allowed portions of the attached window to lie inside the cylinder and filled them by nearest-neighbour remapping. \subsection{Temporal coarse graining and correlated-noise covariance}
To test whether marker fluctuations survive increased sampling, groups of $m=1,2,4,8,16$ consecutive density fields are averaged \emph{before} re-extracting $\scenter$ and $\deltaten$. Let $C_m$ denote the angular covariance of the marker obtained from group size $m$. The attenuation of an AR(1) process with lag-one coefficient $\phi$ under block averaging is
\begin{equation}
 A_m(\phi)=\frac{1}{m^2}\left[m+2\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(m-k)\phi^k\right].
 \label{eq:attenuation}
\end{equation}
We model the covariance as
\begin{equation}
 C_m=A_m(\phi_p)C_p+A_m(\phi_n)C_n,
 \label{eq:covmodel}
\end{equation}
where $C_p$ is persistent physical covariance and $C_n$ is correlated marker/sampling covariance. The 10--90 width channel, which is much more sensitive to local sampling fluctuations, calibrates $\phi_n$; the centre channel then determines $\phi_p$, $C_p$ and $C_n$. The fitted covariance matrices are constrained to be positive semidefinite.

Model preference is assessed by the small-sample corrected Akaike information criterion, leave-one-group-size-out covariance prediction, moving-block bootstrap, far-angle covariance for $|\theta-\theta'|\geq15^\circ$, and matched synthetic positive and negative controls. The physical time scale is
\begin{equation}
 \tau_p^*=-\frac{\Delta \tstar}{\log \phi_p}.
\end{equation}
A physical mode is called resolved only when the two-component model improves out-of-sample prediction, bootstrap model preference is positive, long-range covariance is positive, synthetic false detections remain controlled, and the PSD-constrained fit does not require a large correction.

\subsection{Stationarity, window persistence and control experiments}
A persistent covariance can be inferred only from a record that is sufficiently stationary over the analysis interval. For every full record we test the angular-mean centre and width for a linear trend and compare the variances of the first and second halves using Levene's test. The full $\KnD=0.025$ record gives centre trend and variance-stationarity $p$-values of 0.407 and 0.149, respectively; the corresponding width values are 0.752 and 0.098. The width variance is therefore close to the conventional 5\% threshold but does not reject stationarity. The full records at $\KnD=0.05$, $0.075$ and $0.15$ likewise show no significant centre trend. The $\KnD=0.10$ centre has a weaker margin ($p=0.070$), motivating the window-resolved interpretation used below.

Stationarity of low-order statistics does not guarantee persistence of the inferred mode. We therefore repeat the complete covariance fit in overlapping 192-snapshot windows stepped by 48 snapshots. A window passes only if the physical-plus-noise model has $\Delta AIC_c>10$, improves LOOCV, recovers a mode aligned by at least 0.70 with the $\KnD=0.01$ reference, has positive far-angle covariance and requires less than 20\% PSD correction. This is a substantially stronger requirement than a positive in-sample likelihood gain.

Two synthetic controls complete the calibration. The negative control simulates only the fitted correlated noise covariance and measures false detections. The positive control injects a known rank-one angular displacement with prescribed memory and measures detection power. For the final exclusion analysis, injection amplitude is varied from zero to six times the reference physical standard deviation, and Wilson intervals are used for power. This permits a non-detection to be described as either informative or sensitivity-limited.

\subsection{Full-field displacement-template validation}
The marker analysis is tested against the complete macroscopic fields. For $q\in\{\rho,M,T_{tr},p\}$ we define
\begin{equation}
 \Psi_q(s,\theta)=-g(\theta)\frac{\partial \mean q}{\partial s},
 \label{eq:template}
\end{equation}
where $g(\theta)$ is the normalized physical angular mode inferred at $\KnD=0.01$. The least-squares displacement amplitude is
\begin{equation}
 a_q(t)=\frac{\langle q'(t),\Psi_q\rangle_W}{\langle \Psi_q,\Psi_q\rangle_W},
 \label{eq:amplitude}
\end{equation}
with a weight $W$ concentrated on the density-gradient core and normalized to give each retained ray equal total weight. This construction uses every supported field point rather than one crossing. Moving-block confidence intervals and circular time-shift tests quantify the correlation between $a_q(t)$ and the independently extracted marker amplitude. We also form conditional composites from the largest positive and negative marker events and compare them with the translation reconstruction.

\subsection{Slow-memory and multi-moment synchronization diagnostics}
The translation-template amplitudes permit two additional diagnostics that connect the statistical coordinate to flow physics. First, the regression or coherent displacement gain of moment $q$ relative to the marker is
\begin{equation}
 G_q=\frac{\operatorname{cov}(a_q,a_m)}{\operatorname{var}(a_m)}
 =r_{qm}\frac{\sigma_{a_q}}{\sigma_{a_m}},
 \label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
where $a_m$ is the angular marker amplitude, $a_q$ is the equivalent displacement obtained from the full-field template, and $r_{qm}$ is their correlation. $G_q=1$ corresponds to a field translating with the same amplitude as the marker; $G_q<1$ indicates partial moment participation, additional relaxation or a reduced signal-to-noise ratio. Unlike the projection-energy fraction, $G_q$ retains the sign and amplitude of the coherent component and therefore provides a direct comparison between compression, pressure, velocity--sound-speed and thermal responses.

Second, the four field amplitudes $a_\rho$, $a_p$, $a_M$ and $a_{T_{tr}}$ are standardized and assembled into a matrix $\bm A(t)$. The leading principal-component fraction
\begin{equation}
 S_1=\frac{\lambda_1(\operatorname{cov}\bm A)}{\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{cov}\bm A)}
 \label{eq:synch}
\end{equation}
measures the degree of multi-moment synchronization. A value near unity means that all four moments are organized predominantly by one common temporal amplitude. We also report the median pairwise moment correlation and the correlation of the first principal component with the marker.

The collective memory is compared with two purely geometric convective times. Because $\tstar=tU_\infty/D$, passage across the mean 10--90 width and mean standoff corresponds to
\begin{equation}
 t_\delta^*=\frac{\deltaten}{D},\qquad
 t_s^*=\frac{\scenter}{D}.
 \label{eq:passage}
\end{equation}
The ratios $\tau_p^*/t_\delta^*$ and $\tau_p^*/t_s^*$ distinguish a slow collective response from fluctuations that decorrelate as structures are simply convected across the local layer.

\section{Results}
\subsection{A broadening mean layer with a high-rank stochastic background}
The mean density layer broadens continuously over the low-Knudsen interval (\cref{fig:meanrms}). The half-jump centre changes only modestly between $\KnD=0.01$ and $0.025$, but $\deltaten/R$ nearly doubles. At $\KnD=0.05$ the transition spans a substantial fraction of the radius. In contrast, the raw density RMS remains distributed over the complete compression region and is not concentrated on a single deterministic shape.

At $\KnD=0.05$, a nominal two-component fit can be drawn, but it fails the out-of-sample and PSD-correction tests below.

\subsection{Angular covariance and collective displacement shape}
The physical covariance matrices in \cref{fig:covariance} provide the clearest visual evidence. At $\KnD=0.01$ and $0.025$, broad positive off-diagonal regions connect rays separated by tens of degrees. Their leading modes are same-signed over the complete sector and displace the mean half-jump front with a non-uniform angular envelope. The mode should therefore be described as a collective displacement, not as rigid translation. At $\KnD=0.05$, the matrix is patchy, the PSD correction is 54\%, and the two-component LOOCV error is 3.7 times the noise-only error. That panel is retained only to show the failure of identifiability.

First, pressure, Mach number and translational temperature are projected independently using their own mean gradients; their temporal amplitudes are not used in the marker extraction. \subsection{Dynamic invariance under early mean-layer broadening}
The principal new separation between mean and dynamic behavior is shown in \cref{fig:dynamic}. Between $\KnD=0.01$ and $0.025$, the sector-median 10--90 density width increases from $0.123R$ to $0.224R$, an $82\%$ broadening, whereas the mean half-jump standoff changes by only $1.3\%$. The layer therefore undergoes substantial internal spreading before its centre moves appreciably. In spite of this static change, the inferred angular displacement modes remain almost collinear: their absolute normalized inner product is $0.972$ (the demeaned Pearson shape correlation is $0.885$). Both modes remain same-signed over the complete $120^\circ$--$179^\circ$ sector, although the angular envelope is not exactly uniform. Early rarefaction thus changes the thickness of the compression layer much more strongly than it changes the geometry of the resolved collective motion.

The time-scale comparison in \cref{fig:dynamic}(b) strengthens this interpretation. The inferred memories are $\tau_p^*=0.653$ and $0.724$, while the corresponding layer-passage times are only $t_\delta^*=0.0615$ and $0.112$. Hence the covariance persists for $10.6$ and $6.5$ layer passages. It also persists for $2.83$ and $3.09$ standoff passages. A marker perturbation generated only by local advective crossing or by independent cell noise would not naturally retain a common angular sign for so many geometric transit times. The slow memory supports an interpretation in which the bow layer samples a global, body-coupled displacement direction that is continuously forced by the stochastic kinetic background.

\subsection{Moment-selective dynamic decoherence}
Persistence of the marker shape does not imply that every macroscopic moment continues to translate with equal amplitude. Equation~\eqref{eq:gain} separates this issue. At $\KnD=0.01$, the coherent gains are $G_\rho=0.828$, $G_p=0.781$, $G_M=0.702$ and $G_{T_{tr}}=0.690$. The four moments therefore participate in a nearly common displacement, with density and pressure slightly closer to the marker than Mach number and translational temperature. The first standardized multi-moment component contains $87.3\%$ of amplitude variance, its correlation with the marker is $0.886$, and the median pairwise moment correlation is $0.807$.

At $\KnD=0.025$, the displacement coordinate remains resolved but the participation hierarchy becomes much stronger. The density and pressure gains decrease to $0.631$ and $0.575$, while the Mach-number and translational-temperature gains fall to $0.367$ and $0.312$. The first multi-moment component now contains $70.6\%$ of the variance and the median cross-moment correlation decreases to $0.555$. Thus the front geometry remains collective while the thermodynamic response becomes less synchronous. This behavior is distinct from the static variable-dependent scale hierarchy documented in the companion paper: here the separation is measured in the temporal amplitudes of a common displacement template, not in parameter-dependent mean profiles.

The phase portraits in \cref{fig:phase} make this distinction visible without energy-ranking the fields. Density remains closely slaved to the marker at $\KnD=0.01$ and $0.025$, whereas the translational-temperature cloud broadens and rotates toward weak correlation at $0.025$. At $\KnD=0.05$, neither phase portrait provides a robust common-coordinate signature under the full inference criteria. The physically conservative conclusion is therefore progressive \emph{moment-selective decoherence}: early rarefaction preserves a slow compression-coordinate geometry, but velocity--sound-speed and thermal adjustments increasingly acquire independent fluctuations and relaxation content.

\subsection{What can be concluded beyond $\KnD=0.025$}
A non-detection is meaningful only when the analysis could have detected a mode of relevant size. Sliding-window and injection tests are summarized in \cref{fig:limits}. The $\KnD=0.025$ mode passes the strict window criterion in six of nine independent overlapping windows. At $\KnD=0.1$, only two early windows pass, whereas the complete record is ambiguous. No windows pass at $\KnD=0.05$, $0.075$ or $0.15$ under the combined criterion.

The injection test uses the $\KnD=0.01$ shape and time scale with the measured noise covariance of each case. At $\KnD=0.025$, detection power at the reference amplitude is 0.89 and the interpolated 90\% threshold is $U_{90}/A_{ref}=1.03$. For $\KnD=0.05$ and $0.075$, the thresholds rise to 5.0 and 5.33. At $\KnD=0.1$ and $0.15$, 90\% power is not reached even at six times the reference amplitude. Consequently, the data beyond $\KnD=0.025$ cannot exclude persistence of a low-Knudsen-sized mode. The correct result is resolved emergence at two near-continuum states, not demonstrated disappearance with rarefaction.

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{A slow stochastic coordinate, not a dominant instability}
The analysis identifies two apparently contradictory facts. The instantaneous shock-attached fields are high rank, yet a reproducible angular displacement exists. There is no contradiction because energetic dominance and dynamic organization are different properties. The translation template accounts for only $1$--$5\%$ of raw instantaneous variance, but its amplitude is correlated across distant angles, across independent Knudsen-number records and across multiple macroscopic moments. The coordinate is therefore best understood as a weak slow direction embedded in a much larger broadband kinetic state space.

The separation of time scales is central. At the two resolved conditions the memory survives for $6.5$--$10.6$ passages across the mean 10--90 layer and about three passages across the standoff distance. The response is consequently slower than local profile crossing and is coupled over a large fraction of the bow geometry. This combination of low energy, long memory and broad spatial participation is characteristic of a noise-excited slow coordinate rather than a dominant deterministic oscillation.

No growing DMD eigenvalue was detected in the common-200 sequences, but this empirical observation is not a linear-stability proof. A useful conceptual model is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial q'}{\partial t}=\mathcal L_k q'+\eta(t),
 \label{eq:stochastic_operator}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal L_k$ is a stable kinetic linear operator and $\eta$ is molecular forcing. Even when every eigenvalue of $\mathcal L_k$ is stable, a weakly damped or non-normal response can acquire a finite stationary covariance. In an ideal linear stochastic description that covariance satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_k\bm C+\bm C\mathcal L_k^\dagger+\bm Q=0,
 \label{eq:lyap}
\end{equation}
where $\bm Q$ is the forcing covariance. The present work estimates a projected macroscopic part of $\bm C$; it does not identify $\mathcal L_k$ or $\bm Q$ separately.

\subsection{Dynamic invariance is distinct from mean similarity}
The companion paper \citep{RoohiShoja2026Mean} asks whether the \emph{mean} rarefied bow layer can be represented by one shifted and broadened profile across parameter space. It reports mean inflation, variable-specific length scales and breakdown of universal static similarity. The present result is complementary and non-redundant. Between $\KnD=0.01$ and $0.025$ the mean density width increases by $82\%$, yet the normalized displacement eigenfunction and convective memory remain approximately invariant. The dynamic coordinate is therefore not a trivial restatement of the mean profile collapse. It describes how the instantaneous layer fluctuates about each mean state.

This separation suggests a two-level reduced description. The first level is a slowly varying mean geometry parameterized by rarefaction, as quantified in the companion study. The second is a stochastic displacement amplitude that acts on the local mean gradients. Such a model has the schematic form
\begin{equation}
 q(s,\theta,t;\KnD)
 =\mean q(s,\theta;\KnD)
 -a(t;\KnD)g(\theta;\KnD)\partial_s\mean q
 +q'_\perp,
 \label{eq:twolevel}
\end{equation}
where $q'_\perp$ is the high-rank residual. The present data show that $g$ changes much less rapidly than $\mean q$ between the two resolved Knudsen numbers. This is precisely why a weak coordinate can remain dynamically recognizable even as the mean layer broadens substantially.

\subsection{Moment-selective displacement and relaxation}
The coherent gains reveal a hierarchy that is hidden by marker-only analysis. Density is the variable used to define the half-jump centre, so a strong density gain is expected, but pressure follows almost as closely. This is physically consistent with a compression displacement in which density and translational energy jointly alter pressure. Mach number is more weakly coupled because it depends on both velocity deceleration and the local sound speed. Translational temperature is weakest at $\KnD=0.025$, consistent with thermal adjustment being distributed over a broader relaxation region than the density compression.

The key point is not simply that the correlations decrease. The angular displacement shape remains stable while the relative moment amplitudes separate. Thus early rarefaction does not first destroy the geometric coordinate; it first reduces the degree to which all moments are slaved to that coordinate. We refer to this behavior as moment-selective dynamic decoherence. It is a temporal analogue of multi-scale relaxation, but it is measured from synchronized fluctuation amplitudes rather than from mean thicknesses. The result provides a more specific physical answer than ``the shock becomes diffuse'': the compression geometry remains organized, while thermal and velocity--sound-speed responses acquire increasingly independent dynamics.

\subsection{Relation to kinetic stability theory}
The kLST framework of \citet{Karpuzcu2026} linearizes the Boltzmann--BGK equation about one-dimensional normal shocks and demonstrates that non-Maxwellian velocity distributions shift the eigenspectrum toward less stable regions. Their calculation concerns planar argon shocks at $M_\infty\leq4$ and deterministic spanwise perturbations, whereas the present calculation concerns stochastic, curved, body-coupled nitrogen flow at $M_\infty=10$. Direct equality between their eigenmodes and the present covariance mode would therefore be unjustified.

The two studies nevertheless define a useful next step. Along the stagnation line, one may construct a locally planar kinetic base state and determine whether its least-damped response has a macroscopic projection proportional to $-\partial_s\mean q$. Agreement in spatial shape and decay time would support the interpretation of the measured coordinate as stochastic excitation of a stable kinetic response. Disagreement would be equally informative, indicating that curvature, wall coupling or non-normal forcing is essential. The present displacement template supplies the observable against which such a theory should be tested.

\subsection{Implications for particle-data modal analysis}
The physical-support audit provides a general warning for registered particle fields. A map can be numerically smooth and produce a large singular value while representing repeated extrapolated values rather than fluid motion. Support must be enforced before interpolation, and modal energy must be audited spatially before it is interpreted. This issue is especially acute near curved walls, moving fronts and adaptive point clouds.

A second warning is that finite-particle noise is not white. Snapshot accumulation, particle persistence and adaptive sampling produce correlated feature error. Group averaging after feature extraction would not reproduce the statistics of a nonlinear marker; the fields must be averaged first and the marker re-extracted. The attenuation function in \cref{eq:attenuation} then permits persistent and sampling components to be compared on the same footing. The combined use of coarse graining, out-of-sample prediction, synthetic controls and full-field matched filtering is more informative than applying POD, DMD or SPOD alone.

For reduced modelling, \cref{eq:twolevel} suggests that one should not seek a globally low-rank representation of every instantaneous field. A more efficient strategy is to represent the parameterized mean layer, the slow displacement amplitude and the broadband residual separately. Density and pressure may share a displacement coordinate over the near-continuum range, whereas Mach number and thermal variables require additional latent coordinates or relaxation states.

\subsection{Limitations and scope of the conclusions}
The study uses one body shape, Mach number, wall condition and nitrogen model. Vibrational excitation and chemistry are omitted. Absolute fluctuation amplitudes depend on simulator particle weight, sampling duration and output cadence; they are not universal molecular fluctuation levels. The mode is resolved only at $\KnD=0.01$ and $0.025$. Power analysis shows that the higher-Knudsen records cannot exclude a mode of comparable low-Knudsen amplitude, so the manuscript does not claim disappearance or a critical Knudsen number.

The full-field template is a first-order translation model and explains a small fraction of unconditioned variance. Curvature changes, width fluctuations and moment-specific relaxation can contribute additional coherent coordinates. Direct analysis of velocity distribution functions would be needed to connect the macroscopic coordinate rigorously to a kinetic eigenmode, resolvent mode or collision-mediated relaxation mechanism. Finally, the two-dimensional cylinder omits spanwise variations. Extending the method to three-dimensional bow shocks would require separating azimuthal displacement, corrugation and body-scale global modes.

\section{Conclusions}
Time-resolved DSMC fields of Mach-10 nitrogen flow over a circular cylinder were analysed to distinguish a physical bow-layer displacement from correlated particle sampling fluctuations. The principal conclusions are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item Shock-attached interpolation must respect physical support. Solid-side nearest-neighbour filling produced false leading POD energies of order $40$--$50\%$. After gas-domain clipping, the leading combined-state energy is only $1.1$--$4.3\%$ and 146--172 modes are required for $90\%$ variance.
\item A reproducible physical angular mode is resolved at $\KnD=0.01$ and $0.025$. It is same-signed over the upstream sector, has positive far-angle covariance and retains memory $\tau_p^*=0.653$ and $0.724$.
\item The mean 10--90 density width increases by $82\%$ between the two resolved states, while the angular modes retain an absolute normalized inner product of $0.972$. The collective memory spans $6.5$--$10.6$ layer-passage times and about three standoff-passage times. Static broadening therefore occurs without immediate loss of the slow displacement geometry.
\item The coordinate is recovered independently in the complete density, pressure, Mach-number and translational-temperature fields. Density and pressure retain the strongest coherent displacement gains. As $\KnD$ increases from $0.01$ to $0.025$, the gains of Mach number and translational temperature decrease much more rapidly, and the leading four-moment synchronization fraction falls from $0.873$ to $0.706$.
\item The resulting physics is moment-selective dynamic decoherence: early rarefaction preserves the slow compression-coordinate geometry but weakens synchronous velocity--sound-speed and thermal participation. This temporal result is distinct from the mean inflation and parameter-space similarity reported in the companion arXiv paper.
\item The collective coordinate is weak, explaining only $1$--$5\%$ of raw instantaneous field variance. It is a slow coherent direction embedded in broadband kinetic fluctuations, not a dominant POD/DMD mode and not evidence of linear instability.
\item At $\KnD\geq0.05$, statistical power deteriorates too rapidly to demonstrate disappearance. The defensible result is the existence and multi-moment validation of a slow near-continuum collective coordinate, together with quantitative bounds on its identifiability.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Derivation of the coherent-gain and memory diagnostics}
For completeness, the least-squares slope of a field-equivalent displacement $a_q$ on the marker amplitude $a_m$ is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat G_q=\arg\min_G\sum_t(a_q-Ga_m)^2
 =\frac{\operatorname{cov}(a_q,a_m)}{\operatorname{var}(a_m)},
\end{equation}
which gives \cref{eq:gain}. The metric combines correlation and relative amplitude. It is not an energy fraction and can therefore remain appreciable when the translation template explains little of the total unconditioned variance.

The memory ratios in \cref{eq:passage} use only body-scale convective normalization. At $\KnD=0.01$, $\deltaten/D=0.0615$ and $\scenter/D=0.231$, giving $\tau_p^*/t_\delta^*=10.61$ and $\tau_p^*/t_s^*=2.83$. At $\KnD=0.025$, the corresponding ratios are $6.46$ and $3.09$. These values are descriptive comparisons, not universal constants or fitted scaling laws.

\section{Separation from the companion mean-flow analysis}
The companion manuscript \citep{RoohiShoja2026Mean} uses converged mean fields across Mach- and Knudsen-number sweeps. Its principal observables are mean front location, mean thickness, variable-specific static relaxation lengths, profile registration and POD across operating conditions. The present paper uses consecutive outputs at fixed operating conditions. Its principal observables are temporal marker covariance, autoregressive memory, angular eigenfunctions, full-field equivalent displacement amplitudes, sliding-window persistence and injection-based detectability. No temporal covariance, memory, coherent-gain or multi-moment synchronization result in the present paper is reported in the companion work.
