\begin{abstract}
Time-resolved direct simulation Monte Carlo (DSMC) fields are used to test whether a detached rarefied hypersonic bow shock possesses a slow collective displacement that can be separated from correlated particle-sampling fluctuations. Mach-10 rotationally relaxing nitrogen flow over a circular cylinder is analysed for $0.01\leq\KnD\leq1$. A density half-jump front is extracted on body-normal rays, unsupported solid-side points are excluded, and temporal coarse graining is performed before feature extraction. The marker covariance is decomposed into a persistent autoregressive component and a correlated sampling component, with model selection, block bootstrap, synthetic controls and full-field matched filtering used as independent gates. Corrected field POD is high rank at every Knudsen number, yet a weak, same-signed angular displacement is resolved at $\KnD=0.01$ and $0.025$. Across these two states the mean density layer broadens by $82\%$, while the angular mode shapes remain strongly aligned and their inferred memories are statistically compatible with an order-one convective time. Density and pressure recover the marker motion most strongly; Mach number and translational temperature show weaker participation at $\KnD=0.025$, indicating moment-selective weakening of synchrony rather than immediate loss of the geometric coordinate. The signal is therefore interpreted as a low-pass, noise-excited bow-layer response embedded in broadband kinetic fluctuations, analogous in form---but not in forcing mechanism---to classical stochastic models of shock motion. The present higher-Knudsen records are not sufficiently sensitive to establish physical disappearance.
\end{abstract}
