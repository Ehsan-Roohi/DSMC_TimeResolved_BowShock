Time-resolved DSMC fields contain strong correlated sampling fluctuations, so apparent shock motion can be created by particle statistics or non-physical registration support. We show that a weak, same-signed displacement of a Mach-10 rarefied bow layer survives support correction, temporal coarse graining, out-of-sample covariance prediction and full-field reconstruction at two near-continuum Knudsen numbers. The response behaves as a slow low-pass coordinate embedded in a high-rank kinetic background, while pressure and density remain more synchronized with it than Mach number and translational temperature.
